I build things. Most of them start with a problem I can't stop thinking about.

          
First year Electrical Engineering student in the International Undergraduate Program at ITS. My curiosity sits between hardware and AI, and those two keep running into each other.

          
Before university I spent high school doing research. I worked on EcoMeth, a home biogas system that turns organic household waste into cooking fuel, and FLOBRIC Solar Still, a low cost water distiller for communities without reliable clean water. Neither won. Both taught me how to take a messy real world problem, research it properly, and defend my reasoning in front of judges.

          
Two years running I reached the semifinals of the National Olympiad ELECTRA, hosted by the Electrical Engineering department I now study in. That is where I knew this was the right place for me.

          
Then I started building for real. An offline MP3 player where I designed the analog output stage myself, from the DAC to a custom amplifier for in-ear monitors, and fixed its ground noise with a star-ground rebuild. An agentic AI assistant whose automation layer I designed and wrote myself. An Android player for lossless and hi-res audio with USB DAC support and a custom equalizer. I hold three AI certifications from OpenAI Academy, and I scored 593 on the ITS English proficiency test.

          
Right now I am applying to Anargya, the ITS Formula Student EV team, for the low-voltage and safety division: wiring harness, shutdown circuits, and telemetry. It is the kind of engineering where a mistake means the car does not race, which is exactly why I want it.

          
Sharing this is the part I enjoy most. I regularly pull friends into study and skill building sessions, because learning alone gets boring fast.

HARDWARE

        
Offline MP3 Player

        
Built a portable music player from scratch. ESP32-S3 microcontroller, PCM5102A DAC, a custom NE5532 amplifier stage sized for 8.5-ohm in-ear monitors, 1.3 inch OLED display, and microSD storage for fully offline playback.

        
Working fully on a breadboard, with the firmware in C++: MP3 decoding from microSD, I2S streaming, a spectrum visualizer, and a Wi-Fi file manager. I diagnosed a persistent ground noise problem and fixed it by rebuilding the grounding into a star ground, with analog, digital and power returns joined at one point. AI tools were my rubber duck for the driver and timing bugs that would have cost me days; the fixes were mine. The pocket PCB layout in KiCad comes next.

PCB

        
Step-Down Converter Board

        
Designed and routed a fully through-hole step-down converter in KiCad around the LM2575, with an adjustable output from 1.23 to 9.43 V, a switchable ON/OFF pin, 0.8 mm minimum traces, and a two-layer ground pour joined by via stitching. Verified with ERC and DRC, with the fabrication outputs exported.

AI

        
Agentic AI Assistant

        
Built and run a personal agentic assistant that handles real work: long-term memory, tool use, scheduled automations, and multi-step task execution across terminal, browser, email, calendar and GitHub.

        
Designed the automation layer myself: 6 scheduled jobs backed by 23 Python scripts I wrote (~2,200 lines) that pull from my inbox and calendar, the campus Moodle, Instagram and competition sites, then filter, deduplicate and deliver to the right Discord channel. The LLM is one component in that pipeline, not the author of it.

        
It routes every model call through 9Router, an OpenAI-compatible gateway I run locally that unifies 41 models across 6 provider nodes behind a single endpoint, so it picks the best model per task and fails over between providers. In production it has handled 1,594 LLM requests and ~43.8M tokens across 18 distinct models, plus 22 custom skills developed along the way.

MOBILE

        
MKH Player: Lossless Music App

        
Built an Android player for lossless and hi-res audio, aimed at headphones and external DACs rather than casual listening. Kotlin with Jetpack Compose and Jetpack Media3 (ExoPlayer), background playback through a MediaSessionService, and a Room + DataStore layer for library and preferences.

        
The audiophile side is the point: FLAC, ALAC, WAV and DSD support, a bit-perfect / hi-res status readout, live USB DAC detection and routing, a multi-band equalizer with tuning presets (bass boost, treble, DSP envelope shaping), and a custom visualizer I drew on a Compose canvas and bound to the audio session.

        
I used AI design and coding assistants to move fast, the same way I would use a framework or a smart autocomplete. The feature set, the architecture, the choice of Media3, the state model, and the long iteration until it actually played were mine.

RESEARCH

        
EcoMeth & FLOBRIC Solar Still

        
Two full research projects from high school, both taken from problem to measured result.

        
EcoMeth (Toyota Eco Youth 13): an anaerobic home digester that turns organic household waste into methane biogas and liquid fertiliser, cutting methane emissions (a greenhouse gas 25x more potent than CO2). Started with field surveys at TPS 3R Gunung Anyar and interviews with residents, then compared four waste-handling options and built the one the community chose.

        
FLOBRIC Solar Still (National Scientific Writing Competition XVII, Ministry of Public Works and Housing): a solar water distiller using a floating black fabric wick on polystyrene foam. Five days of experiments showed TDS dropping from 448 to 127 PPM, well inside the 600 PPM safe-drinking threshold, at up to 11.3% efficiency. I measured temperature, mass, TDS and pH, then analysed it all in Excel.

        
Neither won. Both taught me how to research a real problem properly.
